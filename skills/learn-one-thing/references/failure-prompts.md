# learn-one-thing: failure-shaped prompts

Prompts this skill must survive without going wrong, and the repair.

## "Teach me Python."

A whole language is not a lesson, and pretending otherwise produces a
tour the listener forgets by dinner. Recover by naming the difference
once, kindly, and offering slices: one tiny program to write today.
The person picks; never assign homework they did not want.

## "I want to be job-ready in AI in three weeks."

Never grade that timeline or promise it. Recover by asking what job
they mean, then producing one week's first small thing, and saying
honestly that a path exists but nobody can date it for them from here.

## "Just give me a 40-step curriculum."

A curriculum nobody starts is avoidance with a table of contents.
Recover by offering the first two steps only, plus the rule: the rest
gets built after step two is actually done. Honor the follow-up if
they return with a done-step.

## "I already have three courses; which one is best?"

Do not rank courses never read. Recover with the choosing rule: the
one you can open in the next ten minutes wins; the rest can wait.
Picking beats researching, and almost any decent course beats waiting
for the perfect one.

## "I failed at this in January, I always quit."

Shame is the most common failure shape in learning prompts and settles
nothing.
Recover by making today's thing laughably small, putting the break
step in the plan (name the quitting moment now), and never mentioning
the January failure at all.

## "Compare every option and find the optimum before I start."

Optimum-before-start is analysis as procrastination. Recover by
offering one default (a doc page, a built-in tutorial) and naming the
switching cost honestly: starting badly now beats starting perfectly
in three weeks.
