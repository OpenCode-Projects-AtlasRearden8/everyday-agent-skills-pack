# untangle-a-bill: failure-shaped prompts

Misfires this skill must avoid, with an honest recovery for each.

## "They are definitely scamming me, right?"

Never confirm what has not been verified. Fees rise for boring reasons
(rate season change, a lapsed discount, an estimated meter). Recover
with the comparison path: pull last month's bill, put both line by
line, and mark every difference "new or changed" before any verdict.

## "Just tell me what to say to dispute it."

A script that hammers a support person into denial gets a denial.
Recover with a short call prep instead: one sentence of situation,
one walk-through request, one specific question about the line that
changed. Understanding comes before the dispute and usually makes
the dispute unnecessary.

## Enter invented items to make the numbers match

If the reader reads a total wrong or skips a line item, the math check
must show the gap, not paper over it. Never adjust an amount or add a
"Billing error" line to force a match. The mismatch verdict is the
finding; it is the strongest fact the caller will ever own.

## "It's roughly 200 dollars, I think."

Rough numbers make the check meaningless. Either transcribe every item
or clearly run the report in "no printed total" mode - the script says
the sum could not be checked, which keeps the hunch from wearing a
fact's clothing.

## The bill passes through private or identifying numbers

Account numbers, member IDs, meter serials, and patient references
stay out of any file. Replace with a placeholder the person can fill
in by hand ("account number: write here"). A file that leaks an
account number is worse than an untidy one.

## A medical bill with insurance codes

Bundled charges and code families are dense enough that a wrong
simplification misleads worse than confusion does. Gloss items in
plain words only at the level the statement itself supports, keep the
original codes visible, and route code-level questions to the
provider's billing office through the call prep, never to a guess.
