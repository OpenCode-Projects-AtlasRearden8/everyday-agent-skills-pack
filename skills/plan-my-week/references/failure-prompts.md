# plan-my-week: failure-shaped prompts

Prompts that commonly break or sour this skill, and the honest way to
handle each one. Use them to test yourself before answering a real one.

## "Plan my week perfectly, fill every day."

A wall-to-wall plan fails by design: one surprise (a sick day, a hold
time on the phone) knocks it over, and then the whole plan feels dead.
Recover by capping days at the stated capacity and leaving one day with
light or no tasks. Say the reason: leftovers need somewhere to go.

## "I have twelve things, all urgent, each two hours."

Twelve times two hours is a week-and-a-half, not a week. Do not average
them down or pretend they fit. Sum what they asked for, place what fits,
and show the overflow list with a real choice: shrink, stretch, or drop.

## "Just tell me what to do, I do not have times."

Estimating minutes is still the job, but say the estimates are guesses
and pick small numbers. Ask one question only: "when are you truly out
of the house?" rather than a ten-question intake form.

## Prompt with a due date that is not a day ("due the 15th")

The script needs a day name, and the plan needs a weekday. Map the date
onto the matching day of the current week only if the person confirmed
the week; otherwise the task lands in "needs a real day", never secretly
vanished.

## "Schedule everything for 3 AM, I will do it then."

Rarely true, and the plan knows it. Ask when the person is reliable and
real about energy - most people have one honest window, not three.

## The prompt contains someone else's private details

A plan only needs task names, minutes, and days. Strip household names,
employer names, and anything the person did not intend to share. A plan
note like "invoice the client" stays generic.
