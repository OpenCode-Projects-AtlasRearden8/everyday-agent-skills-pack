# prep-my-appointment: failure-shaped prompts

Prompts this skill commonly breaks on, and the honest handling for each.

## "Tell me what the doctor will say and what I should do about it."

That is medical advice, and neither the skill nor an agent can give it.
Recover by writing the question for the professional: "what would make
you concerned about this?" The brief carries questions; answers belong
to the visit.

## "It's at the clinic on Main Street."

There are two clinics on Main Street in many towns. Do not pick one in
the brief. Put "Which clinic on Main Street?" under "still need you to
find this" and keep moving instead of stalling the whole prep.

## "Remind me of everything, then joke that I will forget anyway."

Forgetfulness jokes land as shame, and a flustered reader misses the
bring list. Same facts, warmer frame: the brief is for a person who is
coping well, so no punchline and no scolding, ever.

## The visit is a funeral or a court date

Not every meeting is an interview with a provider. Sections still work,
but rename them in the reply: "bring" stays, "ask" may become "ready to
answer", and "want to leave with" may become "done". The shape bends;
the honesty gap-filling does not bend.

## "Just bring my drugs" (names needed but not given)

Never write medication names into the brief from memory, local slang,
or half-heard words. The honest line is: "bring the bottles or a photo
of each label."

## The prompt includes a real ID number, full birth date, or account number

None of those belong in a brief or its files. Replace each with what the
item is called ("government ID", "case number") and leave the values in
the person's wallet, not in a text file.
