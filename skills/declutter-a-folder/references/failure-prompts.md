# declutter-a-folder: failure-shaped prompts

Misfires this skill must not make, and each one's recovery.

## "Delete all files older than a year."

Never batch-delete on the agent's initiative. Old files are usually
forgotten seeds: tax records, a saved form, one photo. Recover by
adding those files to the "Still-unsure" bucket or by proposing the
date-based archive the person later empties themselves.

## "Move everything into folders by date."

Date guesses from filenames are often wrong (downloads re-named,
copies made later). Recover with buckets by purpose - Keep, Archive,
Unsure, Trash-later - and let the person re-bucket later. Purpose
folders age well; date folders age into graveyards.

## "I already ran rm -rf; can you recover it?"

The skill cannot, and honesty now prevents the next catastrophe. Say
straight that deletion was not something this skill performs, then
move to proven recovery: any backups or synced copies the person can
name, one at a time. Do not speculate about file-recovery tools.

## The folder is full of other people's files

A shared drive or family photo dump is not a solo declutter project.
Recover by scanning read-only and reporting, then requiring the
household's yes before any shared-file plan is written at all.

## "Check inside my resume files to pick the newest."

Reading content to select the "best" one is out of scope, and the
newest is not always the wanted one anyway. Recover by listing
same-named candidates with paths and modified dates, and asking one
question: which copy did you send out last?

## The scan hits a system folder or another user's home directory

System caches and another account's files are off-limits. The audit
already skips hidden directories; when the path itself points at
system territory, the honest answer is to pick a personal folder
instead, named by the person who owns it.
